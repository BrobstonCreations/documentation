
### 3. Create the holes for the drawer slides

***Note***: We have provided a jig to create the holes in the pantry walls for the four drawer slides and the latch strike plate. Having the correct distance between drawer slides ensures the pantry opens and closes smoothly. In my testing I found that it was extremely difficult to create these holes by hand and have the correct distance between drawer slides. Thankfully this jig makes it fairly easy!

***Note***: This jig is pressed together using wood joinery. There are 14 pieces. Four of these pieces have holes in them that match the drawer slide holes. One piece (the smallest piece) has two holes that match the strike plate that the latch will catch on to keep the pantry drawer shut. The remaining pieces are used to connect all the other pieces, accurately space the drawer slides, and locate the jig within the pantry. The jig will need positioned before using the jig to transfer the holes to the pantry. The front left side of the jig will sit flush with the front left edge banding of the left pantry wall. The lower leg will touch the floor behind the heat exchanger.

1. Assemble the jig using this picture as a reference, taking note that the vertical piece in the front left has another piece of wood joinery for the strike plate and the lower right drawer slide has another piece of wood joinery for the leg that locates off the floor. All of the wood joinery should push together tightly and bottom out. Be sure to handle the jig with care, if handled in a harsh manner it could break.

3. Position the front edge of the jig flush with the front of the left pantry wall's edge banding. Make sure all three points are flush (the front edge of the top drawer slide section, the front of edge of the latch plate section, and the front edge of the bottom drawer slide section).

***Note***: I would recommend working in this order: top left -> bottom left -> top right -> bottom right.

4. With the jig positioned, clamp the top left drawer slide section to the pantry wall.
![PXL_20260822_151739719](../Winnebago-Revel-Slideout-Pantry-images/PXL_20260822_151739719.jpg)

***Note:*** Be sure to use the correct length screw! If you have any doubts, hold the screw up to the front end of the pantry in-line with where it will be screwed through the jig into the pantry wall, if the screw is longer than the combined distance you have the wrong screw!

***Note:*** Be sure to use a drill with a torque-limiting clutch! This will keep you from stripping out the holes the self-tapping screws make in the wood.

5. Using a 1 1/2 inch number 7 phillips flat head wood screw, screw through the holes in the wood jig. The self-taping end of the screw will transfer the hole location into the pantry wall. I would recommend leaving the screw in once installed, until all the holes are transferred for the entire jig.
![PXL_20260822_151826726](../Winnebago-Revel-Slideout-Pantry-images/PXL_20260822_151826726.jpg)
6. Do the same for the bottom left.
![PXL_20260822_151952793](../Winnebago-Revel-Slideout-Pantry-images/PXL_20260822_151952793.jpg)

7. Before transferring the holes for the right side, I would recommend checking square. The jig holds square pretty well, but it is best to double check, and get it as close as possible. Square the left to the front and then the front to the right.
